## Chart Justifications

Q1: Which month had the highest total revenue?

A bar chart is appropriate for this univariate analysis, comparing a single variable (total revenue) across three discrete time periods (April, May, June). Bar charts enable viewers to instantly identify the tallest bar (May at ₹444,594.25) within 10 seconds. The y-axis should start at zero to maintain accurate visual proportion. Avoid 3D effects that distort perception. Since only one series is shown (total revenue), no legend is required.

Q2: What percentage share does Ethnic Wear represent of April's total revenue?

A pie chart is ideal for this univariate composition question, showing how one category (Ethnic Wear at 24.92%) fits within the whole (April's ₹419,417.43 total). The slice size immediately communicates Ethnic Wear's proportion relative to other categories within 10 seconds. Pie charts are designed for part-to-whole relationships and do not require a zero-baseline y-axis. Avoid 3D effects; keep it flat for clarity. A single series (April) requires no legend.

Q3: How do the four regions compare on total revenue?

A bar chart is appropriate for this univariate analysis, comparing a single variable (total revenue) across four discrete geographic categories (North, South, East, West). Bars should be ranked by height to enable instant comparison and ranking within 10 seconds. The y-axis must start at zero. Avoid 3D effects. Since one series is displayed (regional totals), no legend is needed.
